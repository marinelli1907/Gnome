# 28 — Facility decision package (home vs storage-only vs flex space)

2026-08-13. Nothing here has been rented, contacted, or purchased. No landlord,
broker, or insurer has been approached, and Daniel's home address appears
nowhere in this repository.

**Recommended launch posture: home-based fulfillment, conditional on three
confirmations** (§6). It is the only posture whose cost matches a pilot that
has not sold anything yet — but "conditional" is doing real work: two of the
three confirmations are one phone call each, and one of them can kill the
option outright.

## 1. What was actually verified

Richmond Heights zoning, read directly from the codified ordinances
([American Legal Publishing](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/richmondhts/latest/richmondhts_oh/0-0-0-2),
local legislation current through **June 23, 2026**):

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| Zoning code location | Part Eleven, Title Five. Single-family districts are **Chapter 1135 (R-1, R-2)** and **Chapter 1139 (R-3)**. |
| **Chapter 1135 has no home-occupation section** | Its complete section list is 1135.01 Intent · 1135.02 Permitted uses · 1135.03 Residential Cluster Development · 1135.04 Yard, area and bulk · 1135.05 Off-street parking · 1135.06 Walls and landscaping · 1135.07 Signs and lighting. There is no "home occupation" or "accessory business use" section. |
| Citywide prohibited uses (1125.09) | Junk yards, automobile trailer camps, tourist cabins; trailers/RVs/tents as dwellings. **Nothing here reaches storing or shipping sealed retail goods.** |
| Short-term rentals (1125.11) | Expressly prohibited in residential districts — evidence that this city legislates residential-use limits explicitly when it intends them. |
| Other relevant chapters | 1123 Definitions · 1169 Conditional Uses · 1173 General Provisions (incl. accessory buildings, 1173.05(k)) · 1175 Off-street parking · 1179 Signs · 1180 Regulated Uses. |
| City contact | City of Richmond Heights, 26789 Highland Rd, Richmond Heights, OH 44143. |

**What this does and does not mean.** The absence of a home-occupation section
is genuinely favorable — many Ohio suburbs cap home businesses with an explicit
ordinance and this one appears not to. It is **not** permission. Whether a
home-based operation is allowed turns on the permitted-use list in **1135.02**
and the definitions in **1123**, neither of which could be retrieved in full
this session (the code viewer served the chapter's table of contents but not
the section bodies). Treat this row as **unverified** until §7 Q1 is answered.

Also relevant from the seed research: the AASCO Ohio entry states Ohio does not
require a permit to *distribute* seed ("permit is to label seed not
distribute") — see [27-final-agency-inquiries.md](27-final-agency-inquiries.md).
That reduces, but does not eliminate, the chance that ODA has facility
expectations of us at all.

## 2. Option A — Home-based inventory and fulfillment

**What it is.** Sealed retail seed packets stored in conditioned living space;
orders packed at home; carrier picks up or packages are dropped off.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | ~$0 incremental. Shelving and a scale, under $200. |
| **Fits the product** | Yes. A 12-packet Drop is a padded envelope. 2,000 packets is roughly two file boxes. |
| **Fits the pilot** | Yes — Ohio-only, low volume, no customer traffic. |
| **Kills it** | A lease/HOA/deed restriction, an insurer declining the business exposure, or a zoning answer that requires a conditional use. |

**Risks that are real, not theoretical**

1. **Homeowner's insurance almost certainly does not cover it.** Standard HO-3
   policies exclude business property above a small sublimit (commonly
   $2,500 on-premises) and exclude business liability entirely. Unaddressed,
   a total inventory loss is uninsured and a product claim is uncovered.
2. **The business address becomes public.** An LLC needs a registered agent
   address, carriers need a pickup address, and an ODA inquiry response goes
   somewhere. Using the home address publishes it. **Mitigation: a commercial
   mailbox with a street-format address (not a PO box) for all public and
   registration use, with the home used only as the physical pack location if
   zoning permits.** Decide this *before* sending the ODA letter in
   [27-final-agency-inquiries.md](27-final-agency-inquiries.md), because that
   letter carries a return address.
3. **Inspection access.** If ODA ever inspects, it inspects where the seed is.
   That is a home. Question O5 asks ODA directly.
4. **Growth ceiling.** Fine at 50 subscribers; awkward at 300; wrong at 1,000.

**Where it stops being viable:** more than ~2 pallet-equivalents of inventory,
any regular non-resident helper, any customer coming to the door, or any
carrier requiring a scheduled commercial pickup.

## 3. Option B — Self-storage for dead inventory only

**What it is.** A climate-controlled 5×10 or 10×10 unit holds reserve stock.
**No packing, no order fulfillment, no business operation inside the unit** —
that distinction is the whole point, because nearly every self-storage lease
forbids conducting business on site.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | ~$70–160/month for a climate-controlled 5×10 in eastern Cuyahoga County (verify current local rates; not quoted this session). |
| **Solves** | Home space pressure; separates bulk reserve from working stock. |
| **Does not solve** | Insurance, business address, zoning at the pack location, inspection access. Those all still attach to wherever packing happens. |
| **Watch** | "Climate controlled" often means heated-only, not humidity-controlled. Seed viability turns on **humidity** more than temperature. Ask for the actual RH range, not the marketing phrase. |

**Lease terms to read before signing:** prohibition on business use;
prohibition on receiving deliveries at the unit; insurance requirement (most
require a rider or sell their own); access hours; the operator's right to
enter; and lien rights over the contents on non-payment — a storage lien over
inventory is a real risk to a business that stores its only stock there.

## 4. Option C — Small commercial flex / warehouse

**What it is.** 500–1,500 sq ft of flex space with a proper business address,
receiving door, and no residential-use question at all.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | Materially more than either alternative — rent plus CAM, utilities, a security deposit, and typically a multi-year term. Obtain real quotes before comparing. |
| **Solves** | Zoning, insurance underwriting, business address, inspection access, growth, employees — all at once. |
| **Costs** | A fixed monthly obligation and a lease term that outlives a pilot which has not yet proven demand. |

**The honest read:** this is the *right* answer at scale and the *wrong* answer
before revenue. Revisit at ~250 active subscribers, when a second person is
regularly packing, or if an insurer will not write the home operation.

## 5. Checklists

### 5.1 Home fulfillment checklist

- [ ] **Zoning** — written or emailed confirmation from Richmond Heights
      Building & Zoning that a no-traffic, no-signage, no-employee shipping
      operation is permitted in the applicable R district (see §7).
- [ ] **Property restrictions** — read the deed for restrictive covenants;
      confirm whether an HOA or subdivision association exists and obtain its
      rules; if renting, read the lease's business-use clause.
- [ ] **Insurance** — a business policy in force before the first packet is
      received (see §8). Do **not** rely on the homeowner's policy.
- [ ] **Business address** — commercial mailbox with street-format address
      secured for registrations, carriers, agency correspondence, and the
      website.
- [ ] **Storage conditions** — conditioned interior space; **no** garage,
      attic, basement floor, or shed. Target cool, dark, dry, RH below ~50%.
      Log a thermometer/hygrometer reading weekly. Store off the floor.
- [ ] **Pest control** — sealed bins, not open shelves; quarterly inspection
      for rodent and insect activity; nothing edible stored alongside.
- [ ] **Security** — inventory in a lockable room or cabinet; regulatory
      documents digital-only in the private `seed-lot-docs` bucket, never in a
      home filing cabinet as the sole copy.
- [ ] **Fire code** — cardboard and paper volume kept low; nothing blocking
      egress; no storage in a furnace/water-heater room or against it.
- [ ] **Carriers** — confirm USPS/UPS residential pickup rules and whether a
      commercial account changes them (see §7 Q6).
- [ ] **No customer traffic. No signage. No non-resident helpers.** Any one of
      these changes the zoning analysis and probably the answer.
- [ ] **ODA access** — a plan for how an inspection would work at a residence
      (question O5 in the ODA letter).

### 5.2 Storage-unit (dead stock only) checklist

- [ ] Lease permits *storage* of business inventory (most do) and you are not
      conducting business there (most forbid it) — confirm both in writing.
- [ ] Genuine humidity control, with the operator's stated RH range.
- [ ] Ground-floor, interior unit; no exterior-facing roll-up door.
- [ ] Insurance: your policy extends to off-premises stock at that address, or
      the operator's rider is purchased.
- [ ] Access hours cover your packing schedule.
- [ ] Read the lien clause.
- [ ] Written inventory-transfer log between unit and pack location — the
      `seed_inventory_log` and `storage_locations` rows must match reality.

### 5.3 Flex-space checklist

- [ ] Zoning permits warehousing/distribution of non-hazardous goods.
- [ ] Certificate of occupancy matches the intended use.
- [ ] Fire inspection and occupancy classification confirmed for storage.
- [ ] HVAC gives genuine humidity control, not just heat.
- [ ] Loading access and carrier pickup suit envelope-and-box volumes.
- [ ] Lease term matched to a pilot, not a decade — 12 months or month-to-month
      if obtainable, with an expansion right.
- [ ] Landlord's insurance requirements and required limits obtained **before**
      negotiating, so they can be priced.
- [ ] Signage rights (Chapter 1179 if in Richmond Heights).

## 6. Recommended launch posture

**Home-based fulfillment for the Ohio pilot**, conditional on:

1. **Zoning confirmation** from Richmond Heights Building & Zoning (§7) —
   one phone call, and it is the only one that can kill the option outright;
2. **A business insurance policy in force** before the first packet arrives
   (§8) — this is not optional and is the most commonly skipped step;
3. **No lease, HOA, or deed restriction** against home business use.

Add a **climate-controlled storage unit for reserve stock only** if and when
home volume exceeds roughly two file boxes — not before, because it buys
nothing except space.

**Defer flex space** until ~250 active subscribers, a regular second packer, or
an insurer declining the home operation — whichever comes first.

If confirmation (1) or (3) fails, the fallback is **not** flex space
immediately: it is a commercial mailbox plus storage unit plus packing at a
lawful business location, priced against a small flex unit before committing.

## 7. Questions for the municipality

Richmond Heights Building & Zoning, 26789 Highland Rd, Richmond Heights, OH
44143. Introduce it as "I want to make sure I do this correctly before I
start" — zoning officials answer that question generously.

1. Chapter 1135 contains no home-occupation section. **Under 1135.02
   (Permitted uses), is a home-based business permitted as an accessory use in
   R-1/R-2 — specifically one that stores sealed retail packaged goods and
   ships them by mail, with no customer visits, no signage, no employees, and
   no outdoor storage?**
2. If it is permitted, is any registration, home-occupation permit, or zoning
   certificate required — and is there a fee?
3. If it is not permitted as of right, is it available as a **conditional use**
   under Chapter 1169, and what is the process and timeline?
4. Are there limits on the **floor area or percentage of the dwelling**
   devoted to the use, or on storage in a garage or accessory building
   (cf. 1173.05(k))?
5. Do delivery vehicles serving a residence create any restriction — frequency,
   vehicle size, or hours?
6. **Does a scheduled commercial carrier pickup at a residence change the
   analysis** versus ordinary residential mail service?
7. Is a **business address at a residence** permitted for state registration and
   commercial mail purposes even where the operation itself is permitted?
8. Are there restrictions specific to **selling goods** from a residence, as
   distinct from providing services?
9. Would the City want to inspect, and does the Fire Department have any
   requirement at these quantities (roughly 2 file boxes of paper packets)?
10. Is there anything about this description that would concern the Department?

Record the date, the person's name, and their answer verbatim. If the answer is
favorable, **ask for it by email** — a verbal zoning opinion is worth little
later.

## 8. Questions for the insurance broker

Describe the business plainly: *an LLC that buys sealed retail seed packets at
wholesale, stores them, and resells them unopened by mail to consumers,
initially from a residence in Ohio.*

1. What policy form covers this — a **BOP**, or a standalone general-liability
   plus inland-marine/business-personal-property combination?
2. **Product liability**: is a reseller of another manufacturer's sealed,
   unaltered goods covered, and are we an additional insured on any supplier
   policy? What limit is appropriate?
3. **Business personal property at a residence**: what limit, and what does the
   homeowner's policy exclude that this must pick up? Confirm in writing that
   the homeowner's carrier will not deny a claim because of the business.
4. Does the homeowner's carrier need to be **notified**, and does notification
   risk non-renewal? (Ask this before notifying.)
5. **Off-premises / storage-unit coverage** if reserve stock moves to a unit.
6. **Goods in transit** once shipped.
7. **Recall expense** coverage — if a supplier recalls a lot we have shipped,
   what pays for notification and replacement?
8. **Errors & omissions / professional liability** for the growing guidance we
   publish alongside the seed.
9. **Cyber / data breach** — we hold customer names, addresses, and order
   history.
10. What do carriers require operationally (locks, alarm, records) to write it?
11. What is the premium at 50 subscribers vs 500 — i.e. when does the flex-space
    comparison change?
12. If we later add bulbs or planting stock
    ([26-roadmap-bulbs-planting-stock.md](26-roadmap-bulbs-planting-stock.md)),
    does the classification change?

## 9. Storage conditions (all three options)

Seed viability is a **humidity** problem before it is a temperature problem.
The working rule of thumb is that temperature (°F) plus relative humidity (%)
should stay comfortably under 100.

- Cool, dark, dry; stable rather than merely cold.
- **RH below ~50%**, logged weekly with a hygrometer.
- Sealed bins; original packets never opened.
- Off the floor, away from exterior walls, out of direct light.
- No garage, attic, shed, or unconditioned basement.
- Nothing edible stored with the inventory (pest attractant).
- Deviations recorded in `seed_lots.condition_notes` — the field exists for
  this, and an honest note beats a clean record.

## 10. Does bringing in help change the analysis?

Yes, on every axis at once. A non-resident helper working at a home converts an
accessory use into something most residential zoning treats differently;
workers' compensation and employment-practices exposure attach; the insurance
classification changes; and both fire and occupancy considerations shift.
**A helper is the trigger to revisit this document, not a detail to absorb.**
Family members already resident are usually treated differently — but confirm
that with the City rather than assuming it (§7 Q1).

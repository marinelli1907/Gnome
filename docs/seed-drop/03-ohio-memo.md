# 03 — Ohio Seed Law & Facility Requirements Memo (Seed Drop)

**Prepared for:** Boone Systems LLC — Gnome Farmers Market / Seed Drop subscription
**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Status:** Research memo — **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Prepared from primary sources for internal planning. Confirm all conclusions marked INTERPRETATION or AGENCY CONFIRMATION REQUIRED with the Ohio Department of Agriculture (ODA) and/or Ohio counsel before relying on them.

**Business model analyzed (locked):** Boone Systems LLC, an Ohio company, buys finished retail seed packets (ordinary vegetable, herb, flower seed) wholesale from established domestic suppliers. Packets stay sealed and unaltered with the supplier's original label — never opened, divided, repackaged, relabeled, or covered. Gnome never claims to be grower, producer, tester, or labeler. Several packets go into a plain padded envelope/box with a Gnome packing slip, sold as a personalized subscription, fulfilled from one Ohio location, shipped direct-to-consumer to cleared states in the contiguous 48 (including Ohio residents).

**Evidence labels used throughout:**
- **EXPLICIT** — the cited text says this directly.
- **INTERPRETATION** — reasoned conclusion from the cited text; not stated in so many words.
- **ACR** — AGENCY CONFIRMATION REQUIRED; ambiguous, get it in writing from ODA.

All statutory and rule text was accessed 2026-08-13 at codes.ohio.gov; all ODA program content was accessed 2026-08-13 at agri.ohio.gov (see Source Ledger and Access Log at the end).

## Executive summary

1. **Ohio's seed labeler permit attaches to the act of *labeling*, not selling.** ORC 907.13: "No person shall **label** agricultural, vegetable, or flower seed that is intended for sale in this state unless the person holds a valid seed labeler permit." ODA's own application form repeats it: "A seed labeler permit is required for each person **labeling** seed to be sold in Ohio." — **EXPLICIT**
2. **Boone Systems, reselling only sealed packets bearing another labeler's original label, does not appear to need a labeler permit** — that conclusion rests on the absence of any seller-permit requirement in ORC 907, so it is an **INTERPRETATION** with **ACR** (get ODA's written confirmation; never operate on "no permit required" without it).
3. **No separate seed dealer, distributor, or warehouse registration exists in ORC Chapter 907** for a reseller of finished packets; the chapter's only seed permit is the labeler permit, and ORC 907.111 bars local governments from adding their own. — **INTERPRETATION** (absence of a requirement) + **ACR**
4. **Seed sales fees and the annual (due Feb 1) seed sales report are duties of permit holders only** (ORC 907.14). The 2%-of-container-value fee category expressly covers vegetable/flower packets of 8 oz or less — payable by the *labeler*, not the reseller. Whether the fee on Boone's resold packets is fully captured upstream is **ACR**. — **EXPLICIT** (who owes) / **ACR** (Boone's residual exposure)
5. **Even without any permit, Boone is a "seller" with real duties:** ORC 907.08(H) forbids selling seed "unless it is labeled in accordance with" the chapter, and ORC 907.07(A) forbids selling seed whose germination test is older than 12 months (36 months if hermetically sealed). Stale test dates on inventory are Boone's problem, not just the supplier's. — **EXPLICIT**
6. **Boone's fulfillment location needs no registration under seed law, but it is a "place of business"** ("any location, including any vehicle, where seed is sold, processed, conditioned, or stored" — ORC 907.01(Q)) that ODA may enter "during regular business hours" to sample seed and copy records (ORC 907.11(A)-(B)). — **EXPLICIT**
7. **Seed law is silent on facility type** — home, self-storage, or warehouse are all permissible *under Chapter 907*; what actually constrains the choice is zoning/occupancy, fire code, the lease, and insurance, which are separate regimes seed law does not govern. — **INTERPRETATION** (silence) + local confirmation required
8. **Ohio law imposes no temperature/humidity/pest standards on sellers**, but storage conditions are legally load-bearing anyway: seed must still meet its labeled germination (within tolerance) and the OAC minimum germination standards when sold, or it becomes unsalable/stop-sale bait. — **EXPLICIT** (the sale prohibitions) / **BEST PRACTICE** (the storage specifics)
9. **Statutory recordkeeping (18 months, lot-level, kept in Ohio) binds permit holders** (ORC 907.04), but ODA may copy "the records of anyone who sells seed" (ORC 907.11(B)) — so Boone should keep 907.04-equivalent records voluntarily. Enforcement tools are stop-sale (907.11(C)), seizure/condemnation (907.12), permit actions (907.15), and misdemeanor penalties (907.99). — **EXPLICIT**
10. **Out-of-state shipments do not escape Ohio law** — "sell" includes "storing, carrying, or handling in aid of traffic in this state" (ORC 907.01(X)), so Ohio requirements apply while packets sit in and ship from Ohio; destination-state seed laws and the Federal Seed Act stack on top (covered in the multistate matrix, not this memo). Subscription selling changes nothing under Chapter 907. — **INTERPRETATION**

## The 14 questions

### Q1. Does Boone Systems need an Ohio Seed Labeler Permit when it resells only sealed packets carrying another (permitted) labeler's original label?

**Short answer: The permit obligation attaches to labeling, not selling — so on the statute's face, no. But because that is a negative inference, treat it as INTERPRETATION and obtain written ODA confirmation (ACR) before launch.**

**The trigger is labeling.** ORC 907.13 (eff. 9/30/2025, HB 96, 136th G.A.): "No person shall label agricultural, vegetable, or flower seed that is intended for sale in this state unless the person holds a valid seed labeler permit that has been issued by the director of agriculture in accordance with this section." (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.13, accessed 2026-08-13) — **EXPLICIT.** ODA's application form (PLNT 4203-008): "A seed labeler permit is required for each person **labeling** seed to be sold in Ohio." (https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/v1771000617/plnt_4203-008.pdf, accessed 2026-08-13) — **EXPLICIT.** ODA's Seed Labeler Permit page: "Required if you: label agricultural, vegetable, or flower seed to be sold in Ohio." (https://agri.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/oda/divisions/plant-health/grain-warehouse-feed-and-seed/seed, accessed 2026-08-13) — **EXPLICIT.**

**"Labeler" is not separately defined.** ORC 907.01 (eff. 4/9/2025) defines "label" — "a tag or other device that is attached to or written, stamped, or printed on any container of seed … that describes the kind of seed together with any other information required by law" — but contains no definition of "labeler" (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.01, accessed 2026-08-13). The labeler is therefore the person who performs the labeling act; ORC 907.03 requires "[t]he name and address of the person who labels the seed" on every agricultural, vegetable, and flower seed label (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.03, eff. 6/15/2006, accessed 2026-08-13). Under the locked model, that name is always the supplier's, never Gnome's. Merely **selling** seed labeled by someone else is nowhere made a permit trigger — no section of ORC 907.01–907.17 requires a seller, dealer, or distributor to hold a permit. — **INTERPRETATION** (conclusion from the absence of a seller-permit provision).

**Three wrinkles to manage:**

- **The outer envelope/box.** ORC 907.01(MM) defines "container" to include "a packet, bag, box, tape, tube, **envelope** … or other device used to contain seed." If Gnome printed seed-descriptive information on its outer box, an aggressive reading could call that a "label" on a "container of seed" — i.e., labeling. The safe reading (and the one consistent with the scheme, since the supplier's packet is the labeled retail container) is that a plain shipping carton with a packing slip *inside* is transport packaging, and a packing slip is not "attached to or written, stamped, or printed on" any container. **Mitigation (already the locked model): keep the outer package plain; the packing slip identifies the order contents and nothing else — no germination, purity, test-date, or origin claims.** — **INTERPRETATION**, flagged in the ODA inquiry (ACR).
- **Public auction is the one sale-side permit hook.** ORC 907.07(I) prohibits selling seed "[a]t public auction unless the seed is labeled in accordance with sections 907.01 to 907.17 … and the person who labeled the seed obtained a permit to do so as provided in section 907.13." (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.07, eff. 10/29/2003, accessed 2026-08-13) Subscription e-commerce is not a public auction — **EXPLICIT** (inapplicable) — but note the drafting: even there, the *labeler* needs the permit, not the auctioneer/seller.
- **Verify every supplier's Ohio permit.** Because suppliers' packets are "intended for sale in this state," each supplier labeling them is the one who must hold the Ohio permit — including out-of-state suppliers (ORC 907.13 expressly contemplates out-of-state applicants, requiring them to list an in-state sampling address or agree to provide samples on request). Boone sells the packets, and ORC 907.08(H) bars selling seed "unless it is labeled in accordance with" the chapter — so an unpermitted upstream labeler creates seller-side risk. **Operational rule: before onboarding a supplier, confirm its current permit in ODA's Seed Labeler Search ("Licensed Seed Labelers are permitted to label agricultural, vegetable, and flower seed for sale in Ohio," data updated June 29, 2026; https://agri.ohio.gov/divisions/plant-health/grain-warehouse-feed-and-seed/online/seed-search, accessed 2026-08-13) and keep a dated screenshot/CSV in the supplier file.** — **INTERPRETATION** of seller exposure; the search tool itself is **EXPLICIT**.

Also note ORC 907.01(K): "'Label' includes an invoice under which any seed is imported into the state." Boone's inbound wholesale invoices are, for imported seed, part of the labeling chain — retain them (see Q12).

### Q2. Any separate seed dealer, distributor, warehouse, or other registration?

**No seed dealer/distributor/warehouse registration exists in ORC Chapter 907.** The chapter index (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-907, accessed 2026-08-13) contains exactly one seed permit — the labeler permit (907.13/907.15) — plus two adjacent regimes:

- **Legume inoculants** (ORC 907.27–907.35): each brand of legume inoculant "distributed or manufactured in Ohio must first be registered with ODA," but per ODA, "a distributor does not have to register the brand if it has already been registered in Ohio by the manufacturer" (ODA Seed Labeler Permit page, accessed 2026-08-13). Relevant only if Gnome ever ships **pre-inoculated** legume seed packets — check supplier registration then. — **EXPLICIT**
- **Treated/colored seed** (ORC 907.41–907.47): poison-treated seed must be dyed and warning-labeled. The locked model should simply exclude treated seed at packet acceptance (cross-reference `08-packet-acceptance-checklist.md`). — **EXPLICIT**

Ohio's grain/commodity handler licensing (ORC Chapter 926, administered by the same ODA program) applies to handlers of agricultural commodities purchased from producers — not to a retailer of finished seed packets. — **INTERPRETATION.**

**Preemption bonus:** ORC 907.111 (eff. 8/21/2014): "The department of agriculture has sole and exclusive authority to regulate the registration, labeling, sale, storage, transportation, distribution, notification of use, use, and planting of seed within the state," and political subdivisions may not require "permits or licenses beyond those mandated by state or federal statute/rule." (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.111, accessed 2026-08-13) So no Ohio city or county can demand a local *seed* license from Gnome. (This does **not** exempt Gnome from generally applicable zoning/occupancy law — see Q6–Q9.) — **EXPLICIT**

Overall conclusion that *nothing else* applies: **INTERPRETATION + ACR** (the inquiry letter asks ODA to confirm no other GFS registration applies).

### Q3. Does Boone Systems owe Ohio seed inspection or sales fees?

**The fee duty sits on permit holders.** ORC 907.14(B) (eff. 9/30/2025): "A **person who holds a valid seed labeler permit** shall include with each annual report a seed fee based on the amount of the seed that the person sold during that reporting period…" (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.14, accessed 2026-08-13) — **EXPLICIT.** If Boone holds no permit (Q1), Boone owes no seed fee under 907.14. — **INTERPRETATION + ACR.**

**The rates (for understanding supplier economics; verbatim from 907.14(B)):**
- Soybeans/small grains: "four cents per one hundred pounds"; corn/grain sorghum: "five cents per one hundred pounds."
- **The Seed Drop category — 907.14(B)(3)(a):** "For any of the following seed sold at wholesale or retail or on consignment or commission, **two per cent of the wholesale value of the containers of seed** or, if the seed is not sold wholesale, two per cent of the retail value…: (i) Vegetable and flower seed sold in containers, other than hermetically sealed containers, of eight ounces or less; (ii) Flower seed sold in hermetically sealed containers that contain fewer than three hundred seeds; (iii) Vegetable seed sold in hermetically sealed containers that contain fewer than one thousand seeds."
- Catch-all: "ten cents per one hundred pounds."
- Floor: "If the total amount of the seed fee that is due is less than fifty dollars, the person shall pay no fee."
- ODA's fee mechanics: "if the total seed sales fees for the year are less than $50, the labeler only pays a $50 permit fee—no additional fees are required. If the seed sales fees are more than $50, the labeler pays the extra amount over $50 plus the $50 permit fee." (GFS landing page and Seed Labeler Permit page, accessed 2026-08-13) — **EXPLICIT**

**Volume-based?** Yes — for packets, 2% of container value; the labeler's wholesale sale to Boone is the natural fee event, so the fee on Gnome's packets should already be embedded in wholesale pricing. Whether ODA sees any residual fee/report duty in Boone's Ohio *retail* sales of those same packets is the sharpest unresolved question — **ACR** (asked verbatim in the inquiry).

### Q4. Annual seed-sales reports required?

**Only for permit holders.** ORC 907.14(A): "A person who holds a valid seed labeler permit … shall report to the director of agriculture concerning the amount of seed that the person sells in this state … prior to the first day of February of each year" for the prior calendar year. — **EXPLICIT.** ODA: "Beginning January 1, 2026 seed labelers are required to report the amount of seed sold in Ohio annually…. All tonnage report forms are generated individually by our office and mailed out to all registrants…. Businesses should not create their own forms." (Seed Labeler Permit page, accessed 2026-08-13) — **EXPLICIT.** No permit → no report for Boone (**INTERPRETATION + ACR**, bundled with Q3). Late/short filing penalty for permit holders: "ten per cent of the amount due or fifty dollars, whichever is greater" (ORC 907.14(C)) — **EXPLICIT** (noted for supplier-diligence awareness).

### Q5. Must the inventory location be registered or inspected?

**Registered: no (for a non-labeler). Inspectable: yes.**

- **No registration.** Nothing in ORC 907 or OAC 901:5-27 requires a seller to register a storage location. Location disclosure is a *labeler-application* element: ORC 907.13 requires out-of-state applicants to list "an address within this state where samples of the applicant's seed may be collected," and OAC 901:5-27-07 (eff. 9/23/2021) requires the application to state "[e]ach Ohio location where seed to be labeled by the applicant may be sampled" (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-901:5-27-07, accessed 2026-08-13). Those bind Gnome's *suppliers*, not Gnome. — **EXPLICIT** (whom it binds) / **INTERPRETATION** (that no seller analogue exists)
- **Inspection exposure is automatic.** ORC 907.01(Q): "'Place of business' means any location, including any vehicle, where seed is sold, processed, conditioned, or stored." ORC 907.01(X): "'Sell' … includes … [s]toring, carrying, or handling in aid of traffic in this state." ORC 907.11(A)-(B) (eff. 10/17/2011): the director may "[e]nter any public or private place of business during regular business hours in order to gain access to any seed or records" and "take samples of the seed or make copies of the records of anyone who sells seed." (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.11, accessed 2026-08-13) **Wherever Seed Drop inventory sits — house, storage unit, or warehouse — is a "place of business" ODA can walk into during business hours.** — **EXPLICIT**

### Q6. Is home-based storage and packing permitted under seed law?

**Seed law neither permits nor prohibits it — it is silent on facility type.** ORC 907 imposes no facility licensing, no facility standards, and no home-business restriction on sellers. A home used for Seed Drop becomes a "place of business" under 907.01(Q) and is subject to ODA entry/sampling under 907.11 during regular business hours — practical implication: someone must be able to grant access and produce records at the home. — **INTERPRETATION** (from silence) / **EXPLICIT** (inspection reach).

**What seed law does NOT govern (separate regimes, separately confirmed):** municipal zoning and home-occupation rules (for a Richmond Heights residence: the Richmond Heights zoning/home-occupation provisions of its codified ordinances — confirm directly with the Richmond Heights Building/Zoning office), building occupancy, fire code, HOA/lease covenants, homeowner's insurance (business-property and liability exclusions), and Ohio sales-tax/vendor licensing. ORC 907.111's preemption covers *seed regulation* only; generally applicable zoning still applies. — **EXPLICIT** (907.111 scope) / flag: **local confirmation required** (not an ODA question).

### Q7. What must a commercial storage/fulfillment location satisfy?

Under seed law, exactly four things — the same four as anywhere else:

1. **Accessibility:** ODA entry, sampling, and record-copying during regular business hours (ORC 907.11(A)-(B)). — **EXPLICIT**
2. **Label integrity:** no detaching, altering, defacing, concealing, or destroying labels (ORC 907.08(A), https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.08, eff. 10/29/2003, accessed 2026-08-13). Storage and pack-out must not damage or obscure supplier labels. — **EXPLICIT**
3. **Sale-condition compliance:** everything shipped must still satisfy ORC 907.07 (test-date windows, labeled values within tolerance, no noxious weed seed) and the OAC germination standards at the moment of sale. Storage conditions are the means to that end (see Q11). — **EXPLICIT** (the sale rules) / **INTERPRETATION** (that they drive facility practice)
4. **Records on hand** in Ohio (statutorily required of permit holders under 907.04; voluntarily mirrored by Boone per Q12). — **EXPLICIT/BEST PRACTICE**

Everything else a commercial suite must satisfy — occupancy classification, fire code, ADA, lease permitted-use clauses, commercial general liability and stock insurance — is non-seed-law and locally confirmed. — flag.

### Q8. Can an ordinary self-storage unit be used ONLY for inventory storage?

**Seed-law view:** Nothing in ORC 907/OAC 901:5-27 forbids it. The unit would be a "place of business" (seed "stor[ed] … in aid of traffic," 907.01(Q)/(X)) subject to ODA entry during regular business hours (907.11(A)) — Boone must be able to admit an inspector past the facility gate and unit lock, and produce records. — **EXPLICIT** (inspection reach) / **INTERPRETATION** (permissibility from silence).

**Honest non-seed-law flags (each can independently kill the idea):**
- **Lease terms.** Standard self-storage rental agreements prohibit conducting business *operations* in the unit and often restrict what may be stored; storage-only may be tolerated where packing is not. Read the actual lease — **a facility's willingness to rent does not establish compliance** with its own lease terms, zoning, or anything else.
- **Zoning/occupancy.** The facility is zoned/permitted as storage, not as a workplace or fulfillment site (see Q9).
- **Climate.** Non-climate-controlled units see summer heat and humidity swings that degrade germination — which converts directly into legal risk: selling packets that no longer meet labeled germination (within tolerance, 907.07(K)) or the OAC minimums (907.08(F)) is a violation. A climate-controlled unit mitigates but does not eliminate this. — **BEST PRACTICE** driven by **EXPLICIT** sale rules.
- **Insurance.** Self-storage facility insurance and typical tenant policies cover personal property, not commercial inventory; business inventory generally needs its own policy/rider.

**Verdict: storage-only in a climate-controlled unit is defensible under seed law (INTERPRETATION), but only after lease, zoning, and insurance check out — and packing should not happen there (Q9).**

### Q9. Is packing/shipping orders FROM a self-storage unit permitted?

Four regimes govern; seed law is the *least* restrictive of them:

1. **Seed law (ODA):** no facility rule prohibits packing in a storage unit; inspection access still applies. — **INTERPRETATION** (silence)
2. **Local zoning/occupancy (the controlling regime):** self-storage facilities are approved as storage occupancies; regularly working inside a unit (assembling orders, staffing it as a workspace) is an occupancy/use question under the local zoning code and the Ohio building/fire codes as locally enforced. For a Richmond Heights (Cuyahoga County) location, this must be confirmed with the Richmond Heights Building Department and Fire Department before any packing occurs in a unit; for a unit in another suburb, that municipality's officials. Expect the answer to be no. — **local confirmation required** (this memo cites no local ordinance and asserts none)
3. **Contract:** the rental agreement's business-use prohibition (Q8) typically bars exactly this.
4. **Business/tax:** Ohio vendor's-license and sales-tax situs questions ride on where sales activity occurs — separate memo; carrier pickup policies (USPS/UPS) are commercial, not legal, constraints.

**Practical structure that stays clean: store sealed cases in the unit (if lease/zoning/insurance allow), pack at the licensed/permitted business location (home with a confirmed home-occupation allowance, or a flex suite), and treat the unit as dead storage only.** — **BEST PRACTICE**

### Q10. When is a commercial flex/warehouse suite effectively required?

No Ohio seed-law threshold ever *requires* one for a non-labeler seller. — **INTERPRETATION** (silence). It becomes effectively required when any of the following is true (judgment / **BEST PRACTICE**):

- Home-occupation limits (floor area, employees, customer/carrier traffic, signage, storage of stock-in-trade) in the residence's municipality are exceeded or packing at home is disallowed;
- Self-storage lease/zoning bars business activity and no compliant home base exists;
- Inventory needs controlled temperature/humidity at a scale a house can't provide (germination-window compliance across a multi-season catalog);
- Non-household labor is hired (workers' comp, occupancy, egress);
- Insurers require a commercial premises to write inventory/liability cover;
- Boone ever becomes a **labeler** (repackaging, own-brand packets, seed added to kits under Gnome's name): then ORC 907.13/OAC 901:5-27-07 require a listed Ohio sampling location, and a proper commercial premises becomes the natural answer.

### Q11. Storage practices — what Ohio law actually REQUIRES vs best practice

**REQUIRED (all EXPLICIT, all seller-side):**
- **Test-date freshness at sale.** ORC 907.07(A): germination test completed within "a twelve-month period prior to sale" (non-hermetic; 15 months for cool-season grass — inapplicable here) or "a thirty-six-month period prior to sale … if the seed is in hermetically sealed containers"; after that, sale only after retest + relabel — which only a permitted labeler can do, so for Boone stale stock is simply **unsalable** (return or destroy).
- **Labeled values must hold.** ORC 907.07(K): no sale of seed "out of compliance with the tolerance established for it"; OAC 901:5-27-02 (eff. 9/23/2021) adopts the AOSA "Rules for Testing Seed" (Oct. 2019) for sampling, testing, and tolerances (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-901:5-27-02, accessed 2026-08-13).
- **Minimum germination standards.** ORC 907.08(F): no sale of vegetable or flower seed in containers ≤8 oz below the director's germination standard unless labeled below-standard. Standards: OAC 901:5-27-04 (eff. 9/21/2014) for vegetable/herb (e.g., lettuce 80%, tomato 75%; "The germination standards for all vegetable and herb seed not identified in this rule shall be fifty per cent") and OAC 901:5-27-08 (eff. 9/21/2014) for flower seed (species table; https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-901:5-27-04 and .../rule-901:5-27-08, accessed 2026-08-13).
- **Label integrity.** ORC 907.08(A): do not "[d]etach, alter, deface, conceal, or destroy any label."
- **No noxious weed seed / weed-seed limits / inert-matter limit.** ORC 907.07(D), (G), (H) (supplier-side in practice, but the sale prohibition binds the seller).

**NOT required by Ohio seed law (no temperature, humidity, pest-control, cleanliness, security, or segregation standard for sellers appears anywhere in ORC 907 or OAC 901:5-27):** the following are **BEST PRACTICE**, adopted because they are how a seller keeps the required outcomes true:
- Cool, dry, dark storage (target ≤70°F and low RH; avoid attics/garages/non-climate units);
- FIFO rotation keyed to each lot's **test date**, with a hard pull-date calendar (12-month non-hermetic / 36-month hermetic);
- Pest monitoring (rodents/insects), off-floor storage, sealed totes;
- Physical security and access control (also serves inspection-readiness);
- Inventory separation: quarantine incoming stock until the acceptance checklist (`08-packet-acceptance-checklist.md`) clears it; segregate any stop-sale/recalled/stale lot physically and in the system.

### Q12. Recordkeeping, inspection, recall, stop-sale, and disposal procedures

- **Records.** ORC 907.04 (eff. 10/29/2003) binds "[a]ny person who **holds a valid seed labeler permit** … and who sells" seed: complete records of each lot "for a period of **eighteen months** from the date of the final sale or other final disposition"; keeping "an invoice of each purchase and sale stating the kind of seed and the lot number" satisfies the duty where suppliers keep full records; records "shall be maintained at a location in this state"; ODA may audit to verify sales "within or into this state" and fees owed (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.04, accessed 2026-08-13). — **EXPLICIT** (binds permit holders). Boone, as a non-permit-holder, has no 907.04 duty (**INTERPRETATION**), but ORC 907.11(B) lets ODA "make copies of the records of anyone who sells seed," and ORC 907.01(K) makes import invoices part of labeling — so **Boone's SOP: retain purchase invoices (kind + lot number), supplier permit verifications, and order-level records mapping each shipped order to packet lot numbers, for 18+ months, at the Ohio location.** — **BEST PRACTICE** mirroring the statute; also the only way to execute a recall.
- **Inspection.** ORC 907.10 (eff. 10/29/2003): the director may "[s]ample, inspect, analyze, and test agricultural, vegetable, and flower seed sold for sowing purposes, at such times and places and to such extent as the director regards necessary." ORC 907.11(A)-(B): entry to any place of business during regular business hours; sampling; record copying. — **EXPLICIT**
- **Stop-sale.** ORC 907.11(C): written stop-sale order to "the owner or custodian of any lot of seed, requiring it to be held at a designated place"; the lot is frozen "until a release is issued in writing"; moving, handling, disposing of the lot, or touching its labels while under order is itself a violation (ORC 907.08(C)); appeal per Chapter 119. **SOP: on receipt of a stop-sale, freeze the lot in place, halt fulfillment of any order containing it, and call GFS.** — **EXPLICIT**
- **Seizure/condemnation.** ORC 907.12 (eff. 10/29/2003): noncompliant seed may be seized on the director's complaint; on condemnation it may be "denatured, destroyed, or otherwise disposed of," but not before the claimant gets "an opportunity to apply to the court for the release of the seed or for permission to process or relabel it" into compliance. — **EXPLICIT**
- **Recall.** ORC 907 contains **no consumer-recall regime**; stop-sale and seizure are the statutory tools. Customer-level recall capability (which subscriber got which lot) is voluntary — **BEST PRACTICE**, enabled by the order-to-lot records above.
- **Disposal.** The only disposal rule is for processors' screenings: ORC 907.08(G) and OAC 901:5-27-05 (eff. 9/23/2021: no disposal/burning of screenings "in a manner that may result in poisoning, discomfort, or nuisance … or in the dissemination of weed seed") — inapplicable to Boone, which never processes. — **EXPLICIT** (inapplicable). Disposal of Boone's own stale/damaged packets is unregulated by seed law (absent a stop-sale/condemnation); return-to-supplier or destruction such that seed won't be resold is **BEST PRACTICE**.
- **Penalties.** ORC 907.99 (eff. 6/20/1994): violations of 907.01–907.17 = misdemeanor of the fourth degree (first offense), third degree thereafter. Permit-holder reporting penalties: ORC 907.14(C). Permit suspension/revocation/refusal: ORC 907.15 (eff. 10/29/2003), with Chapter 119 hearing rights and 30-day appeal. — **EXPLICIT**

### Q13. Does the analysis change when packets ship OUTSIDE Ohio?

**Ohio's grip does not loosen; other regimes are added.**

- **Ohio scope.** ORC 907.01(X): "'Sell' or 'sold' includes: (1) Transferring ownership, offering or exposing for sale, exchanging, distributing, giving away, or **transporting in this state**; (2) **Storing, carrying, or handling in aid of traffic in this state**…" Every Seed Drop shipment — wherever bound — is stored, handled, and transported in Ohio, so ORC 907's sale prohibitions (labeling, test dates, germination standards) apply to all of it. — **INTERPRETATION** (application of the explicit definition). Jurisdiction/enforcement venue: ORC 907.17 (eff. 10/2/1974). The labeler-permit trigger is likewise keyed to seed "intended for sale **in this state**" (907.13); packets bound only for other states arguably sit outside that phrase for the *supplier's Ohio permit* analysis — but since suppliers sell to Boone *in Ohio*, their permits are needed regardless. — **INTERPRETATION**
- **Ohio's fee/report base is Ohio sales only.** ORC 907.14(A) reports cover "the amount of seed that the person sells in this state" — a permit-holding labeler's fee base, not Boone's concern absent a permit. — **EXPLICIT**
- **Interstate transport carve-out.** ORC 907.09(A)(3) (eff. 4/9/2025) exempts from 907.03/907.07/907.08 "seed that is in interstate transport and that is governed by 7 C.F.R. 201.33" — i.e., federally governed seed moving in interstate commerce; it does not exempt Boone's Ohio-based selling activity. (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-907.09, accessed 2026-08-13) — **EXPLICIT** (existence) / **INTERPRETATION** (limited reach)
- **What stacks on top:** the **Federal Seed Act** (7 U.S.C. 1551 et seq. / 7 C.F.R. 201) for interstate shipments — satisfied in practice by shipping supplier-labeled, unaltered packets, but it is federal law outside this memo's scope — and **each destination state's seed law** (several states license seed *dealers/sellers*, unlike Ohio). **Destination-state clearance is governed by the multistate matrix (see `docs/seed-drop/matrix/`), not this memo; no state may be marked "cleared" on Ohio analysis alone.** — flag
- **907.09's other exemptions** (not-for-sowing; unprocessed seed to processors; non-commercial seed sharing/seed libraries) do not fit a paid subscription: Seed Drop seed *is* for sowing and *is* compensated, so 907.09(A)(4)'s "non-commercial seed sharing" (defined at 907.01(OO) as transfer "with no compensation or remuneration") is unavailable. — **EXPLICIT**

### Q14. Does subscription selling (vs ordinary retail) change anything under Ohio law?

**Under Chapter 907: no.** The chapter regulates "sell[ing]" — defined without regard to sales format (907.01(X)) — and nothing distinguishes subscriptions, clubs, or recurring fulfillment from shelf retail. — **INTERPRETATION** (from silence + breadth of "sell").

What the subscription's *marketing* does trigger (same as any retail seller, but worth engineering into Gnome copy review):
- **ORC 907.07(C):** no sale of seed "[p]ertaining to which there has been a false or misleading advertisement." — **EXPLICIT**
- **ORC 907.08(B) + 907.081 (eff. 10/29/2003):** any advertised claim about "vigor, vitality, growth, yield capability, or any other quality or performance characteristic" requires maintaining "complete and accurate records of the tests used to determine the characteristic" and producing them to the director on request; unsubstantiated claims are prohibited and can ground a stop-sale. **Rule for Gnome copy: describe varieties using the supplier's labeled data and neutral horticultural information; make no performance claims Gnome cannot substantiate with test records.** — **EXPLICIT**
- **ORC 907.06** (eff. per current text; title: "Words prohibited which imply state guarantees"): avoid any implication that ODA inspection/permitting is a state guarantee of the seed. (Cited by title from the chapter index; full text not separately pulled — do not paraphrase further without reading it.) — noted, **verify text before relying**
- Auto-renewal disclosures, negative-option billing, and Ohio Consumer Sales Practices Act compliance are **consumer-protection law, not seed law** — separate workstream. — flag

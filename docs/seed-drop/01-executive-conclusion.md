# Executive conclusion — can the Seed Drop fulfillment model launch as written?

2026-08-13 · Boone Systems LLC / Gnome Farmers Market · Not legal advice.

## The answer in one paragraph

**Yes, the model is fundamentally sound — nothing found anywhere blocks
reselling unopened, originally-labeled retail seed packets — but it cannot
launch nationwide today.** No federal license exists for it, no state prohibits
it, and 15 states are already cleared by explicit authority. What stands
between here and broad launch is (1) one unresolved federal labeling question
that touches every interstate shipment, (2) written confirmations in 18 states
where the law is ambiguous for this exact model, (3) about 15 inexpensive state
registrations, and (4) a supplier-credential audit that several states make
load-bearing. The verdict is **LIMITED-STATE PILOT REQUIRED** — full reasoning
in [24-launch-verdict.md](24-launch-verdict.md).

## The five facts that matter most

1. **The federal crux is real and unresolved.** 7 CFR 201.27(c) defines the
   interstate shipper of vegetable seed as "the seller or consignor who puts
   the seed into interstate commerce" — that is Boone Systems, whose name is
   not on the packets. Whether the supplier's name satisfies the requirement,
   whether the outer parcel/packing slip can carry it, and whether a small
   supplemental (non-covering) Boone sticker is permitted are precisely the
   questions drafted for USDA AMS SRTD ([02-federal-memo.md](02-federal-memo.md)).
   Until answered, every interstate shipment carries this open question.
   Ohio-to-Ohio shipments do not — which is what makes an Ohio pilot possible
   first.
2. **The FSA covers less than assumed.** Flower seed has no federal Part 201
   labeling regime at all, and herbs count as vegetable seed only if named in
   the 201.2(i) list. State law does most of the work for flowers/herbs — and
   state coverage varies, so catalog classification must be per-state.
3. **Ohio's permit attaches to labeling, not selling.** The ORC 907.13 labeler
   permit almost certainly does not reach a reseller of another permitted
   labeler's sealed packets — but that is a negative inference, held at
   AGENCY CONFIRMATION REQUIRED with a drafted ODA letter. Real Ohio seller
   duties exist regardless: verify each supplier holds a current Ohio labeler
   permit (907.08(H) puts sell-side liability on Boone), and never sell past
   the 12-month (36 hermetically sealed) germination-test window
   ([03-ohio-memo.md](03-ohio-memo.md)).
4. **The 48-state picture is manageable and nothing is BLOCKED:**
   **15 CLEARED** (AZ DE IA ID KS MA MD MI MT ND NJ NY RI UT WI) ·
   **15 REGISTRATION REQUIRED** (AL CA FL GA IL IN MO MS NC NH OK PA SC SD WV —
   roughly $10–$150/yr each, ~$450–650/yr total at launch volumes) ·
   **18 AGENCY CONFIRMATION REQUIRED** (each with a send-ready inquiry).
   The recurring structural finding: in at least eight states lawful resale
   depends on the **supplier** holding that state's labeler credential — a
   one-time supplier-credential audit unblocks several states at once
   ([04-state-matrix.md](04-state-matrix.md)).
5. **The product is specifiable today and the economics work above the
   4-packet tier.** The full V1 spec (sizes 4/8/12/custom 4–20, four control
   modes, packet-scale honesty model, Richmond Heights journey, admin,
   fulfillment, tests, database plan) is complete in this pack. Unit economics
   show ~37–43% contribution margins at reference price midpoints, with the
   4-packet tier postage-burdened and the current $24.99 price underwater in
   the high-cost scenario ([16-pricing-economics.md](16-pricing-economics.md)).
   Final prices await real supplier quotes.

## What must NOT happen yet

Nationwide enablement · applying Seed Drop migrations · Stripe changes ·
inventory purchases · agency contact (drafts only) · supplier agreements ·
publishing final prices · representing any unresolved state as cleared.
